A living agent for codebases

It grows into the shape of your problem.

Agent 01 is a small organism that lives inside your repository. Give it a task and it puts out a limb, tests it against your code, and either thickens the limb or pulls it back. Nothing about the shape is fixed in advance.

Feed it a task

or just move your cursor into it

01 What it eats

Context is food, and it is picky about freshness.

The organism takes in four kinds of tissue and refuses everything else. It will not swallow a file it has not opened, and it will not swallow a file that changed since it last read it — a stale limb is worse than no limb.

  • Source. Whole files, imports resolved, revision hashed on entry.
  • Tests. Read before the code under test, so the limb knows what “working” means.
  • Issues & notes. Your own words, given the same weight as the code.
  • Run history. What it tried last week and why that failed, kept locally.

Digestion is local. No tissue leaves the machine. The only thing that ever leaves is the diff you approved, and only when you push it somewhere yourself.

02 Capabilities

Six limbs. One body. It decides which to grow.

The organism starts whole. When a task demands it, it buds into limbs, each doing one job, then pulls them back into the body. Hover or focus a limb below and watch it swell in the field beside you.

03 Membrane

A membrane keeps it in one directory, for one run.

The organism cannot leak. It runs inside a membrane made of three walls: your working directory, a token budget you set, and a transaction that either lands whole or rolls back. Poke a hole in any wall and it stops moving.

1directory, mounted read-write, nothing above it
400ktokens of context, then it stops and reports
1command to undo a run: agent01 revert

Nothing is written until you approve a limb, and an aborted run leaves no scar tissue behind. If a test fails after an approved edit, the agent reports the failure in the same breath as the success — the log has no hiding place.

04 Touch it

Feed it something small first.

Give it a failing test, or a function nobody understands any more. It grows a limb, shows you the diff, and waits. Nothing touches your tree until you say yes.
